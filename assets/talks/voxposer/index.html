Reconstruction: the setup

Recover the poses of two known dominoes from one saved tabletop observation.

  • Scene: two rigid dominoes on a table. Reconstruct only these objects.
  • Observation: RGB, aligned 3D points from FoundationStereo, and saved object masks.
  • Human input: one pixel per domino and a clear rectangular patch of the table.
  • Frame: fit using the table plane, then express both poses in the camera frame.

This is an offline reconstruction of a static capture. A robot controller is not running.

Source bundle exp_20260917_141030/look_01/bundle/30264679. The capture metadata identifies FoundationStereo as the XYZ source and supplies K_scaled. The selected pixels are grey (400, 340) and green (505, 390); the smallest saved masks containing them are indices 22 and 2. The clear table region is [540, 390, 680, 470]. Its measured plane RMS is 0.30 mm. The script reads saved fastsam_masks.npz and does not run segmentation again; that filename alone does not establish whether SAM2 refinement was used upstream. This run uses no camera-to-robot extrinsic transform. The recent robot-camera recalibration is therefore not applied here. Original image checksum and camera records are in sources/world-models/reconstruction-figure-inputs.json.

Reconstruction: the workflow

  1. Select the objects. Choose each saved mask from its clicked pixel. Reuse the archived segmentation and depth.
  2. Prepare the 3D points. Measure a table-aligned frame from the clear patch. Erode object-mask boundaries and filter invalid or distant depth points.
  3. Fit known domino geometry. Try several initial poses and minimize distance to the box surface. Constrain the bottom to touch the table.
  4. Resolve the visible side. Compare thickness-shifted alternatives against the surrounding 3D cloud to choose which side of the observed face the box occupies.
  5. Export and check. Transform poses back to the camera frame, generate RoboTL, compile to MuJoCo, and compare the rendered view with the observation.

SDF means signed distance to the box surface. The fitting constraint does not weld the exported bodies to the table.

Canonical owners are BabyRobot markerless.domino_fit.mask_to_base_points, fit_domino_pose and scene.fit_dominoes.resolve_side. Mask erosion is two pixels; foreground filtering keeps camera-depth points within 0.10 m of the fifth percentile, with at most 4000 points. Contact fitting optimizes x, y and two constrained orientation angles, deriving vertical position from known geometry and table height. resolve_side compares candidates shifted by plus or minus one thickness along the box normal and requires improved full-cloud support. The runner snapshot is from commit ef07da9; canonical function source hashes are recorded in the code download. This is numerical fitting and deterministic program export, not LLM program synthesis or MPC.

LLM reconstruction: the setup

An LLM agent writes the RoboTL program of an articulated object, its parts and joints, from RGB-D views.

  • Benchmark: 37 PartNet-Mobility objects in Real2Code's part-count bins. Real2Code did not release its test set, so we render one to its protocol.
  • Input: nine RGB-D views of one articulation state, with cameras. Either no masks, part masks from our fine-tuned SAM2, or the ground-truth part masks.
  • Agent: effectful, with a Python REPL and image tools. Every scene it writes is compiled and scored on silhouette and depth.
  • Budget: 8 scenes and 30 minutes per object. The host picks the best scene on three held-out views.
  • Metrics: Real2Code's own Chamfer distance and joint errors, run from their code.
Real2Code's Drive folder for its synthetic set is empty, so robotl-reconstruction renders 37 test objects from UMPNet's PartNet-Mobility URDFs following their blender_render.py: 12 views of 512 by 512 RGB-D from their front arc at 4 m, hinges in the top 60% of their range, drawers opened at least 80%, MuJoCo rendering with mean texture colors. Their Table 2 bins are 15, 9, 6 and 7 objects; the paper's text says 35 but the bins sum to 37. Real2Code also uses depth: its part_segmentation/sam_to_pcd.py lifts SAM masks to point clouds with rendered depth, and its real objects come from a LiDAR iPhone. Nine views are for fitting and three are held out for selection. Without masks the silhouette comes from valid depth and the agent may call SAM2 itself. Joints are compared in world frame; parts are paired by least total Chamfer distance. Renders carry each part's mean texture color; the first runs rendered 35 of the 37 objects flat gray because trimesh refused the textures' relative paths, and later runs use the fixed renders. Validation views, ground truth and the source dataset stayed off each search machine until every search had ended, and a trace audit found no access to them.

Part masks from surface points

We fine-tuned SAM2 on 12.6k rendered views of 289 PartNet-Mobility training objects, following Real2Code.

The training curve is held-out point IoU, measured every 2000 training views on one twentieth of the training objects kept out of training. A last check after both passes gives 0.90, the best score, so that decoder is the one used. SAM2 is prompted at 100 points drawn evenly from each fitting view's own surface, one point at a time. Prompts whose masks agree, mean IoU above 0.7 over the views they share, form one part, and each pixel goes to the smallest part that claims it. This is Real2Code's test-time scheme. Only the mask decoder was fine-tuned, with Real2Code's recipe of 16 point prompts per image and a BCE, Dice and IoU-head loss, on 12.6k views of 1396 articulation states of 289 PartNet-Mobility objects outside the development and test sets: two passes on rolly's RTX 4090, held-out point IoU 0.54 to 0.90. Median part IoU: SAM2 on the agent's own boxes 0.53; stock SAM2 at surface points 0.79; fine-tuned 0.95; tight boxes around the true parts 0.97. Each is the median IoU of each visible true part against its best predicted mask, over 1241 visible parts across the fitting views of all 37 test objects for the point prompts, and over the 132 visible parts in the 36 views where GPT-5.4 drew boxes for the box prompts. The fine-tuned segmenter over-splits, about 6.8 parts per object against about 3.6 true; this chest gets 9 parts for 6. The agent gets these masks as its segmented-masks condition.

LLM reconstruction: the film

  • Cameras: three of the nine fitting cameras, 48 frames each at 256 by 256.
  • Motion: every joint moves at once, from the observed state to one stop, to the other and back, resting at each stop.
  • Masks: our fine-tuned SAM2 tracks each part's mask from the first frame through the film.
  • Evidence: at about a thousand pixels per camera and every frame, which part shows and how deep it is.
The film starts at the fitting views' state, so the first frame is one of the views the agent already fitted. The hand that moves the joints is not rendered: parts move as if pushed, and each rests against its stop for some frames, which is what pins a limit. The three cameras are view_0, view_4 and view_8 with the fitting views' poses. Tracking is the SAM2 video predictor on the first frame's part masks; across the 37 objects the tracks keep a median IoU of 0.92 against the true part masks at the first and last frames, 5% are lost by the end. The fit's likelihood, on 32 independent rays per frame, scores each pixel's part label and depth against the compiled scene posed at candidate joint positions, under a model where each joint follows a smoothly moving hand clipped to its limits, filtered exactly over a grid of hand positions and speeds.

LLM reconstruction: what the agent writes

self.door = Slab([0.02, 0.4, 0.4], [0.8, 0.3, 0.2, 1.0])
self.door_joint = connect(
    self, self.door, Pose(pos=[-0.354, -0.061, 0.4], rv=[0.0, 0.0, 0.8])
)
self.drawer = Slab([0.36, 0.34, 0.12], [0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0])
opening = choose(dist.TruncatedNormal(-0.15, 0.02, low=-0.3, high=0.0))
self.drawer_joint = connect(
    self,
    self.drawer,
    Pose(pos=[opening, 0.0, 0.11]),
    kinds={"slide": 0.8, "hinge": 0.1, "weld": 0.1},
    axis=dist.ProjectedNormal(20.0 * jnp.array([-1.0, 0.0, 0.0])),
)
constrain(non_overlap(self.drawer, self.shelf))

Excerpt of the example the agent reads. Full source: Download

  • choose: a value the compiler's solver settles, the agent's guess as its prior.
  • constrain: a loss the solver scores its draws by; non_overlap keeps two parts clear.
  • Params: the earlier film contract. The agent writes the joint itself and declares its limits with priors, range=(p.door_closed, p.door_open), for the film to fit.
  • connect: which part moves against which, at the pose fitted to the views. Kind, axis, pivot and limits come from the film; the priors are optional.
  • No prompt text describes these. The agent reads their docstrings and the example in its REPL.
The code is the drawer and door of connected_cabinet_example in robotl-reconstruction (PR 12), the example the agent is pointed at. choose and constrain are RoboTL operations: solve_init samples every chosen value with NUTS and keeps the draw with the highest log density, the constraints entering as factors; a plain float is never moved. Params with Prior is the contract of PR 8: a frozen dataclass of limits the agent declares, read with parameters(Params), each a joint position with a prior wide enough for the film. connect(parent, child, pose, kinds, axis, pivot) is a marker operation: until a fit solves it the scene compiles with the connection welded at pose, so a connected scene scores as its welded twin; kinds is a probability per joint kind, axis a ProjectedNormal over directions in the parent's frame, pivot a Normal over points. The agent's module binds connect, choose, constrain and numpyro's distributions in its REPL scope, so its first cell can print their source; in the pilot it printed connect's and both cabinet examples'.

LLM reconstruction: what the solver does with connect

  • Track each part. Build the scene with every connection free, then align each part's surface to its tracked points in every frame.
  • Read the motion. Each child's poses in its parent's frame, relative to the observed state.
  • Propose three joints. A weld; a slide, its axis from the shifts; a hinge, its axis from the turns and its pivot from where the turns meet. Each with the best limits on a 16 by 16 grid.
  • Choose by the film. The likelihood of every frame under each proposal, less a penalty per parameter, plus the agent's priors.
  • Keep what the views prefer. The scene's own pose stays where the still views score it better; then fit the limits.
  • Out: kind, axis, pivot, limits and both ports. 47 s per connection in the pilot.
Mechanism from PR 12 of robotl-reconstruction. The probe builds every connection as an unlimited slide so each part is its own MuJoCo link; alignment is point-to-plane ICP with trimming distances of 10, 5 and 2 cm, each frame starting from a constant-velocity prediction, with restarts around the observed pose. The motion is X = P_0^-1 T_0 and M_t = (P_t^-1 T_t) X^-1 in the parent's frame. The slide axis is the main direction of the shifts; the hinge axis is the main direction of the rotation vectors and its pivot solves (I - R_t)(c - o) = M_t o - o; both are MAP estimates under the call's priors. The choice is the exact hand filter's log marginal likelihood at the best of a 16 by 16 grid of limits, less a BIC penalty with n = frames times 32 rays, plus the log priors relative to the uninformative defaults; a weld reads the slide's table at position 0. Connections are chosen one at a time over two passes. The still-view check compares the fitting views' silhouette IoU and depth under the solved pose and the scene's own, connection by connection. The controlled check on the five-drawer chest, its joints replaced by connect at poses 3 cm and 10 degrees off, recovered five slides with axes within 0.62 degrees and limits within 6 mm in 661 s; the pilot's fit took 236 s because the agent's kind prior left only slides to score.

Articulated YCB tools

Excerpts of robotl/examples/ycb_artifacts.py at 23dfe34b, the full models

RoboTL PRs 739 to 742. Each tool is cut from its YCB scan into parts on MuJoCo joints. Drill: trigger slide 8.5 mm with a return spring from 1 N at rest to 20 N at its stop (Omron C3AW), switch closes at 2 mm, a centred direction button locks it. Wrench: jaw geared to the worm at 3.5 mm per turn, self-locking. Scissors: pivot with friction, stop at 103 degrees. Spray bottle: trigger hinge to 46 degrees, 14.5 N at a third of its stroke and 29.4 N at its end (P&G US8322630B2). Clamps: Harbor Freight's published openings 44.5, 63.5 and 88.9 mm, clamping force 93.4 N on the medium clamp, Bessey figures for the other two.

Tasks: pick up, then actuate

  • Drill: press the trigger past its switch (2 mm), as far as possible
  • Wrench: close the jaw one worm turn (3.5 mm), then reopen it
  • Scissors: open to 30°, then close to 5°
  • Spray bottle: hold it upright and pull the trigger a third of its stroke, one spray
  • Spring clamps: pick up
  • Each goal held 5 s while the hand carries the tool
Both methods are judged by the same RoboTL objective, scripts/actuate_mpc.py on claude/articulated-tasks 32254ccb: the pickup hold (contained, at least 95% of the weight on the hand, at most 5% outside support, 3 cm whole clearance) plus each stage's joint goal, in order, each held 5 s. A part driven more than 1 mm or 2 degrees past a stop counts as broken, so a jammed trigger never reads as a press. The drill fixture uses 16 hulls per part so a finger can reach the trigger face, and starts with the button at a side.

Can the LEAP hand press the drill trigger?

  • Index push 5.79 N, switch needs 5.47 N, full travel 20 N
  • The pad reaches only the bottom of the trigger, 48 mm from its knuckle
  • Switch never stays closed; the drill turns 12° in the hand

Measurements · Grasp · RoboTL #744

Static check plus simulation, no learning. Each LEAP servo is capped at 0.28 N m, the XC330-M288 at the 0.55 A the LEAP API writes. Press capacity is a linear program over the index's torque box through the contact Jacobian: 5.79 N without friction, 7.81 N with full pad friction. The hold margin while pressing is 5.5, so holding is easy. The simulated press applies those torques directly to the index joints: the trigger peaks briefly, then the drill turns 10 to 12 degrees in the hand and the pad slides off; the switch is closed for none of the 2 s hold. Two fingers do not fit on the trigger. A co-designed hand would need the index 35 to 40 mm higher relative to the palm's top edge, or a palm-hinge lever of 34 mm or less.
