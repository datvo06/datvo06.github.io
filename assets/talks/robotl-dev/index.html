Pickup search

  • A grasp is 15 parameters: the wrist offset and orientation relative to the object's grasp point (x, y, z, yaw, pitch, roll), 4 finger angles shared by all three fingers, 4 thumb angles, and an opening fraction
  • The controller executes it through 4 IK wrist waypoints, 1.5 s apart: 15 cm above the grasp point with the hand open, at it open, at it closed, then 15 cm above it closed
  • One grasp serves every placement: the search does not tune per placement
  • Search is the cross-entropy method, repeated for 8 to 12 generations:
    1. Draw 32 grasps from a Gaussian over the 15 parameters, centred on the current estimate (initially a given seed grasp)
    2. Simulate each grasp on the 4 training placements and sum the loss being searched (next slides); a grasp whose arm cannot reach a waypoint is discarded
    3. Move the Gaussian's mean and spread to the 6 lowest-loss grasps; keep the best grasp seen so far

How the hand picks up the apple

Stock hand, searched grasp; one 8 s episode, frames at the waypoint times

  • Every LEAP joint is a position servo: kp 3 N m/rad, torque capped at 0.28 N m, as on the real hand
  • The fingers close toward the grasp's closed angles, the apple stops them first, and the leftover angle error becomes a squeeze (peak 12.36 N in training)
  • That squeeze, times friction 0.7, carries the 180 g apple when the arm lifts

What counts as a pickup

  • Each placement runs one 8 s native MuJoCo episode at a 5 ms step; the last 2 s are the hold
  • 4 training and 8 held-out placements, shared by apple and hammer: x 0.37 to 0.43 m, y within 3.5 cm, yaw up to ±2.8 rad, tilt up to 0.2 rad
  • Pass: object at least 10 cm above its start for the whole hold, hand contact in 99% of hold samples
  • No robot-floor contact all episode, no object-floor contact during the hold
  • At most 3 mm object-hand penetration, at most 0.02 rad joint-limit violation

Co-designed: spacer hand, refined loss and grasp

Staged native control

  • A stage advances once its goal has held for its dwell (0.1 to 0.25 s; the hold stage needs 2 s) in the live simulation, counted from stage entry; it fails on its deadline or a safety check
  • Deadlines: approach 2.0 s, descent 1.6 s, grasp (thumb and a finger both loaded) 2.4 s, lift to 10 cm 4.0 s, hold 2 s
  • Safety checks every tick: no floor contact, Panda joint speed 2.175 to 2.61 rad/s, acceleration 7.5 to 20 rad/s²; commands projected against actuator range and Panda velocity limits
  • Same apple controls, 12 s replay: wrist peak acceleration 117 rad/s² at a 5 ms step, 13 at 2.5 ms
  • Hammer pilot, a handwritten height term on top of waypoint tracking, 2 samples per replan: descent times out at 3.315 s

class GraspPlan(BoundedDensePlan):
    """Preserve the arm seed while sampling independent hand commands."""

    arm_actuators: tuple[int, ...] = struct.field(pytree_node=False)

    def with_params(self, params: jax.Array, spec: EnvSpec) -> Self:
        indices = jnp.asarray(self.arm_actuators)
        params = params.at[..., indices].set(self.x[..., indices])
        return super().with_params(params, spec)

  • Arm seed: a quintic blend from the issued command to the close waypoint over 1.5 s

Contact sensors and contact-directed objectives

  • Contact sensor: per body pair, a fixed number of contact slots (32 in pickup), each with force, distance, position and normal
  • Virtual sensor: a value computed from other sensor readings
  • Grasp check: thumb and fingers each hold half the object's weight by friction, with normals at least 120 degrees apart
  • Agent losses that read only the fixed base are refused; one such loss read zero all episode
  • More than 32 contacts in a pair: refused, not scored
  • No successful hammer pickup yet

Driving OPH with two XL430 motors

  • Two XL430-W250 at 11.1 V: 1.4 N m stall, 57 rpm
  • Each motor pulls one tendon mixer: motor 1 all distal flexors; motor 0 the thumb and index middle joints and thumb opposition
  • Tendons pull only, with fixed moment arms; gains are assumed, not calibrated

Palm-up OPH

5 cm cube on the palm, two-axis wrist, motors behind the palm. Task: turn the cube to a target orientation in 6 s

OPH morphology variants

gpt-6-astra proposed 6 hands; selection prefers fewer drops, then lower orientation error

  • Held-out random target, controller seeds 11 and 22, 6 s, MPC with a 1 s horizon, orientation loss with an angular-velocity damping term
  • Selected: 95.05 degrees final error, dropped 1 of 2. Base: 28.03 degrees, dropped 2 of 2 (same base in the loss study: 12.00 degrees, dropped 0 of 2)
  • No clear improvement over the base

The Stretch Dex wrist

  • Hello Robot Stretch RE1 Dex wrist: geometry, inertias and joint frames from the published URDF
  • Yaw XL430 with a 2.4 reduction, pitch XM540, roll XM430
  • Drives are documented approximations; the pitch current limit is not modeled

OPH on the Stretch wrist

  • Bracket: a plate on the wrist flange, a column to the palm, shelves under both motors
  • Motors set 12 mm back so the spools clear
  • The printed bracket is an unvalidated prototype

Co-designing the mount

  • Admitted only if the mount meets the flange, palm and motors seat on it, mount and palm are one piece, and the hand clears the wrist by 1 mm on a sampled joint grid

Selection and capture-first loss

4 training and 4 held-out random targets, controller seeds 11 and 29, 1 ms step, 6 s episodes

  • Keep a design only if both orientation errors (episode mean and final) drop, drops do not rise, and at least one training run keeps the cube
  • New loss: chase orientation only once both mixers pass 0.45 rad, thumb and fingers carry load, and the cube is within 4 cm of its hold position
  • A closure term pulls both mixers toward 0.8 rad, 20x stronger until the cube is supported
  • Retention run in progress; capture-first has two single-seed comparisons so far
