Pickup search

  • A grasp is 15 parameters: the wrist offset and orientation relative to the object's grasp point (x, y, z, yaw, pitch, roll), 4 finger angles shared by all three fingers, 4 thumb angles, and an opening fraction
  • The controller executes it through 4 IK wrist waypoints, 1.5 s apart: 15 cm above the grasp point with the hand open, at it open, at it closed, then 15 cm above it closed
  • One grasp serves every placement: the search does not tune per placement
  • Search is the cross-entropy method, repeated for 8 to 12 generations:
    1. Draw 32 grasps from a Gaussian over the 15 parameters, centred on the current estimate (initially a given seed grasp)
    2. Simulate each grasp on the 4 training placements and sum the loss being searched (next slides); a grasp whose arm cannot reach a waypoint is discarded
    3. Move the Gaussian's mean and spread to the 6 lowest-loss grasps; keep the best grasp seen so far

How the hand picks up the apple

Stock hand, searched grasp; one 8 s episode, frames at the waypoint times

  • Every LEAP joint is a position servo: kp 3 N m/rad, torque capped at 0.28 N m, as on the real hand
  • The fingers close toward the grasp's closed angles, the apple stops them first, and the leftover angle error becomes a squeeze (peak 12.36 N in training)
  • That squeeze, times friction 0.7, carries the 180 g apple when the arm lifts

What counts as a pickup

  • Each placement runs one 8 s native MuJoCo episode at a 5 ms step; the last 2 s are the hold
  • 4 training and 8 held-out placements, shared by apple and hammer: x 0.37 to 0.43 m, y within 3.5 cm, yaw up to ±2.8 rad, tilt up to 0.2 rad
  • Pass: object at least 10 cm above its start for the whole hold, hand contact in 99% of hold samples
  • No robot-floor contact all episode, no object-floor contact during the hold
  • At most 3 mm object-hand penetration, at most 0.02 rad joint-limit violation

Contact sensors and contact-directed objectives

  • Contact sensor: per body pair, a fixed number of contact slots (32 in pickup), each with force, distance, position and normal
  • Virtual sensor: a value computed from other sensor readings
  • Grasp check: thumb and fingers each hold half the object's weight by friction, with normals at least 120 degrees apart
  • Agent losses that read only the fixed base are refused; one such loss read zero all episode
  • More than 32 contacts in a pair: refused, not scored
  • No successful hammer pickup yet

def jit_for(instance, fn, **jit_kwargs):
    jitted = vars(instance).setdefault("_jit_methods", {})
    key = (fn, tuple(sorted(jit_kwargs.items())))
    if key not in jitted:
        jitted[key] = jax.jit(functools.partial(fn, instance), **jit_kwargs)
    return jitted[key]
  • The rollout packers and SamplingPlanner compile per instance, so the code goes with its layout or planner; concrete rollout inputs call the host directly
  • Panda MPC on polly, 40 replans: memory plateaus at 15 GB with #649; without it the run is killed at 30 GB after 17 replans

How the agent got there

Read from polly's Langfuse trace: code it ran and status notes it wrote (reasoning summaries came back empty)

  • Step 9: thumb load is zero all episode. The starting loss enables orientation only once every digit group carries 0.1 N and the cube stays within 4 cm of its start
  • Steps 11-27: six thumb relocations and one loss tweak, all dropped the cube; three layouts refused for collisions or a disconnected mount
  • Step 28: an opposed thumb on the added palm bar gets thumb and finger contact 26% of the time; middle and ring knuckles bend backward
  • Step 29: three intermediate flexors on a stronger mixer 0. The first design to keep the cube on both targets; support 42-58% of each episode
  • Steps 30-31: the grip pulls the cube more than 4 cm from its start, keeping the gate shut. The agent writes the hand-relative loss: the selected pair
